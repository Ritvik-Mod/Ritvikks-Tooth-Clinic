Does a root canal really hurt? A microendodontist explains

The most common reason people delay root canal treatment is fear of pain. Here's what modern root canals actually feel like — and why the fear is outdated.

If you have been told you need a root canal and your first reaction was dread, you are not alone. The phrase "root canal" has been shorthand for pain for decades. The reality is very different.

What modern root canals actually feel like

With local anesthesia, the procedure itself is largely painless. Most patients report that having a root canal is no more uncomfortable than having a routine filling — sometimes less. The persistent pain that brought you to the clinic in the first place is typically what makes the experience memorable, not the treatment.

Why the fear persists

The reputation comes from a different era. Before modern anesthesia, microscope-assisted technique, and laser disinfection, root canals were difficult procedures. They sometimes took multiple long visits and could leave teeth tender for weeks. That is not the procedure being performed today.

What you should expect afterwards

Mild tenderness for 1–2 days is normal. Most patients manage with over-the-counter painkillers. Severe pain after a properly done root canal is uncommon — if it happens, contact your dentist.

The real risk is waiting

Putting off a needed root canal allows infection to grow. The tooth can become unsalvageable, and the infection can spread into surrounding tissue. Acting early gives the highest chance of saving the tooth comfortably.
