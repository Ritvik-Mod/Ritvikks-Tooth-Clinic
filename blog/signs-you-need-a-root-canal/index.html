Signs you might need a root canal

Five symptoms not to ignore — and what to do if you notice them. Catching infection early gives the highest chance of saving the tooth.

Some dental problems are obvious. A root canal need isn't always one of them — symptoms can come and go, mimicking less serious problems. Here are the five signs to take seriously.

1. Persistent or severe tooth pain

Sharp, throbbing, or spontaneous pain — especially when chewing — often means the nerve inside the tooth is involved. If pain wakes you up at night or comes without an obvious trigger, see a dentist.

2. Lingering sensitivity to hot or cold

Brief sensitivity is normal. But if pain lingers for more than 30 seconds after eating something hot or cold — or if cold air alone triggers pain — the nerve may be inflamed.

3. A pimple-like bump on the gum

A small, recurring bump on the gum near a tooth is often a sign of chronic infection draining from the root. It may or may not be painful, but it indicates a serious problem.

4. Visible swelling in the gum or face

Swelling is a sign of active infection. Significant swelling needs urgent care — especially if it's spreading, raising your temperature, or making swallowing difficult.

5. Darkening or discoloration of a single tooth

A tooth that has darkened compared to its neighbors — especially after trauma — often indicates the nerve has died inside it.

What to do

Don't wait. Early treatment is more comfortable, more predictable, and more likely to save the tooth.
